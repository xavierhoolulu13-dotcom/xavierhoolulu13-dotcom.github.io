XKSH808 - Notes from the Shop

Plain talk about getting found online, from Honolulu. Short posts, no jargon.

  • 2026-10-21
    Your Website Didn't Break. The Name Expired.

    The website didn't break. The name it lives at expired. You go to send somebody the link. You type it in yourself first, just to check. And it's not your shop. It's a page of ads. "Related Searches." A button offering to sell you the name back. Nothing got hacked. Nothing got deleted. The name.

  • 2026-10-20
    The Screen Is Stuck. Hold the Button Down.

    The screen freezes. Maybe on a photo. Maybe on a text you were halfway through. Maybe on nothing at all — just a white page. You tap it. You tap harder.

  • 2026-10-19
    You Don't Learn It. You Just Tell It.

    Every piece of software I've ever bought wanted me to learn its words first. Fields. Tabs. A dropdown with forty choices, and none of them mine. I don't have the time. Neither do you.

  • 2026-10-18
    The Picture of Your Shop Is From a Day You Were Closed

    Somebody's deciding whether to drive over. They pull up your listing. There's a picture of your shop. Shutter down. Lot empty. Flat grey light.

  • 2026-10-17
    You Don't Have a Website. You Have a Facebook Page.

    Somebody hears about you. Types your name into their phone. Up comes a Facebook page. Maybe it's yours. Maybe it's one somebody made for you years ago. They tap it. And Facebook asks them to log in first.

  • 2026-10-16
    It Went Dark While You Were Reading

    You're reading something. Maybe a text from your daughter. Maybe the instructions on a bill. Halfway down the screen. Right when you get to the important part. It goes dark. You tap it. It comes back. You find your place. You keep reading.

  • 2026-10-15
    You Closed at Six. It Didn't.

    You flip the sign. Lock the door. Drive home. Dinner. Shower. Some TV. Meanwhile the day's still not finished. Somebody left a review at 8. A reminder needs sending. The follow-up you meant to write.

  • 2026-10-14
    Your Listing Says Permanently Closed. You're Open.

    Somebody looks you up. Your name comes up. And under it, in plain words: Permanently closed. You're not. You're right here. Same door, same hours. But they don't know that. They see closed, they believe it, and they move on to the next place.

  • 2026-10-13
    The Little Circle Spins. They've Already Left.

    You know the little circle. It spins while a page loads. Somebody types your name into their phone. Taps your shop. The circle starts spinning. One second. Two. They're standing on the sidewalk with somewhere to be. So they hit back. And call the next place. Your site isn't broken. It's just slow.

  • 2026-10-10
    Your Hours Are Right on the Door. They're Wrong Online.

    The hand-written hours are taped to your door. You update them when they change. Holidays, early close, whatever. Somebody checks before they drive over. Not the door. The phone. And the hours they find are last year's. Closed at six. You're open till eight.

  • 2026-10-09
    It Looks Fine on Your Laptop. It's a Mess on Their Phone.

    You built your website at the kitchen table. On the laptop. It looked good. But that's not where they're looking. They're on the sidewalk. On a phone. One hand. Sun in their eyes.

  • 2026-10-08
    The Words Are Small. You Can Make Them Bigger.

    You're holding the phone out at arm's length. Trying to read the menu. It's not working. Your eyes aren't the problem. The words are just small.

  • 2026-10-07
    It Forgets, On Purpose

    Everything I need to remember goes in my notes app. Phone numbers. A half idea. What somebody told me at the counter. It just sits there. It doesn't lift a finger on its own. And that's fine — it's a notebook.

  • 2026-10-06
    Your Listing Says You Sell Something Else

    Somebody wants what you sell. They type it in. You don't come up. Not because you're missing. You're on the map. You've got reviews. Your pin's in the right spot.

  • 2026-10-05
    Your Website Says Not Secure. Right Next to Your Name.

    Somebody looks you up. Your name comes up. And right there in the address bar, in front of your shop's name, it says: Not Secure. They don't know what that means. They just know it sounds bad. So they back out and call the next place.

  • 2026-10-04
    Your Phone Dings at 2 AM. Here's How to Stop It.

    Your phone goes off at 2 in the morning. A ding. A buzz on the nightstand. You're awake now. It's almost never anything. A store you bought from once. A news alert. Somebody's group text.

  • 2026-10-03
    Nobody Takes a Two-Hour-a-Week Job

    There's a job in your shop that takes about two hours a week. You can't hire for it. Nobody wants two hours a week. And by the time you'd explain it, train them, check the work — you've spent the two hours anyway.

  • 2026-10-02
    The Map Sends Them to the Wrong Block

    Somebody's looking for you. They pull up the map and hit directions. It takes them two blocks over. A parking lot. Somebody's driveway. They look around. No shop. They give up and go somewhere else. You never hear about it. Nobody calls to say your pin's in the wrong place. They just don't come.

  • 2026-10-01
    You Changed Your Number. Your Website Didn't.

    Somebody's phone breaks. Water, drop, whatever. So you get a new number. You tell your friends. You tell your regulars. You tell the guy who does your insurance. You forget the website. That number on your contact page? Still the old one. Somebody else has it now. Maybe a stranger. Maybe nobody.

  • 2026-09-30
    Your Phone Isn't Broken. It's Full of Photos.

    Your phone says "Storage Almost Full." It's not broken. It's just full. And it's almost always photos. Here's the thing nobody tells you. You don't have to delete them. Deleting is the scary part, so people freeze and do nothing.

  • 2026-09-29
    The Back Office Fits in Your Pocket Now

    Nobody opens a shop because they love paperwork. You open it to make the thing. Poke. Cuts. Coffee. Whatever's on your sign. Then the day fills up with the other stuff. The invoice. The reminder. The follow-up. The post you meant to write.

  • 2026-09-28
    The Review You Never Answered Is Still Talking

    Somebody leaves you a review. Three stars. "Food was great. Waited a while." You read it. You feel it. You close the app. That review is still there. And now it's not just talking to you. It's talking to everybody deciding whether to walk in.

  • 2026-09-27
    Somebody Tried to Hire You. The Message Went Nowhere.

    A guy fills out the form on your website. Phone. Thumb. Hits send. He sees "Thanks! We'll be in touch." Then he waits. Nobody calls. So he moves on to the next shop. You never saw it. The form has been quietly broken for months.

  • 2026-09-26
    If It Rushes You, It's Not Your Bank

    Somebody reads you a text. "Your account is locked. Tap here to verify." Is it real? Here's the test I hand out. It works almost every time. Read it out loud. Slowly. If it sounds like it's rushing you, it's not your bank.

  • 2026-09-24
    You're Not on the List

    Watch somebody look for lunch. Phone out. Two words. "Poke near me." Three names come up. Maybe four. That's the whole list. You're not on it. Nothing wrong with your food. Nothing wrong with your door. You just never claimed the little map card with your name on it. Hours nobody updated. No photos.
